# 2. Interviews

**A worked example.** The interviews, quotes and figures below are invented. See `README.md`.

9 to 13 November 2026.

## Why this step

The kickoff gives the sponsor's view of the problem. The people doing the work know where the time really goes, which claims are hard and why, and the rules nobody wrote down. Interviews find the problem in their words; sitting with them while they work checks what they said against what they do.

Ask about the work, never about the people. The aim is to give the team time back, and they should hear that from the start.

## Who we spoke to

| Who | How | When |
|---|---|---|
| Head of Claims Operations (the owner) | One-to-one, 60 minutes | 9 November |
| Senior Claims Handler | One-to-one, 45 minutes | 10 November |
| The two Claims Handlers | Together, 45 minutes | 10 November |
| Claims Finance Manager | One-to-one, 30 minutes | 10 November |
| All three handlers | Sitting with them while they worked, two days | 11 and 12 November |
| Head of Claims Operations | Playback of what we found, 30 minutes | 13 November |

## What we asked

**The owner**

1. Walk me through a claim from the moment it arrives to the moment it is paid.
2. Which claims take longest, and why?
3. How do you know a decision was right? Who checks, and how often?
4. Do two handlers ever decide the same claim differently?
5. What do you measure today? What would you like to measure?
6. What written guidance do the handlers use?

**The handlers**

1. Show me the last claim you decided. What did you open, in what order?
2. Which claims are easy? Which ones do you dread?
3. What do you know now that you wish you had known in your first month?
4. When do you refer a claim instead of deciding it?
5. What happens when a document is missing?
6. If you had an extra day a week, what would you spend it on?

**Finance**

1. How does a payment get released once a claim is approved?
2. What checks happen before money goes out?
3. What does a wrong payment cost to recover? What does a wrong decline cost?

## What we heard

**The work is mostly routine.** The owner estimated that two claims in three are straightforward: clearly covered or clearly excluded once the wording is read. The handlers agreed.

> "Most of my day is reading a delay claim, checking the carrier's letter, adding up receipts and taking off what the wording doesn't pay. It's careful work, but it's the same careful work every time."
> Claims Handler

**The hard claims need the manual.** The first data request, handed over at the kickoff, asked for the claims, the records and the policy wording, but not the claims manual. In the interviews it became clear that the handlers decide many claims from the claims manual, which records how UnderCover reads the wording: what counts as unattended, why a monkey stealing a phone is accidental loss and not theft, and how to pay meals without receipts.

> "The wording tells you what's covered. The manual tells you what we actually do when a claim doesn't fit the wording. New starters learn it the hard way."
> Senior Claims Handler

**Some checks are mechanical.** The 90-day filing window, the 60-day look-back for pre-existing conditions, the third claim in 12 months, the per-item limits and the deductible are all rules with numbers. The handlers work them out by hand or with the payout spreadsheet.

**Referrals are where the judgement is.** A claim is referred when a document is missing, when the story and the documents disagree, or when it is a customer's third claim in a year. The handlers said they never decline for a missing document; they ask for it.

**Decisions may differ between handlers.** The owner believed two handlers would decide most claims the same way, but had never checked. Nobody reviews a sample of decisions today.

**Finance worries most about wrong declines.**

> "An overpayment is money we usually write off. A wrong decline comes back as a complaint, sometimes to the ombudsman, and it costs far more in time and reputation than the claim was worth."
> Claims Finance Manager

Finance confirmed the $1,000 sign-off limit: above it, a handler signs off before payment, after the deductible and the limits. Every handler can sign off.

**What the handlers would do with more time:** call customers whose claims are stuck, work the referred claims faster, and train new starters.

## What sitting with them showed

Over two days the three handlers decided 50 claims between them. What we saw matched what they said, with two additions:

- Much of the time on a claim goes on finding things: the policy in one system, the customer's history in another, the wording in a shared folder.
- Handlers check the customer's claim history on every claim, but nothing prompts them to; it relies on habit.
